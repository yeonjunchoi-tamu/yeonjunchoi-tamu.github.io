Research

Materials, magnetism, and the local probe.

A short overview of current and prior research directions — across solid-state NMR, soft magnetic composites, and electromagnetic shielding materials.

I  ·  Texas A&M University 2024 — present

Solid-state NMR of functional oxides

At TAMU, my work uses nuclear magnetic resonance as a local probe of magnetic and ferroelectric oxides. NMR is uniquely sensitive to the electronic and structural environment of individual nuclei — making it a powerful complement to bulk magnetic and dielectric measurements.

Combined quadrupole and magnetic relaxation

In many of the oxides we study, both quadrupolar coupling and magnetic hyperfine interactions contribute to the observed T₁ and T₂ relaxation. Disentangling these channels is essential before relaxation data can be interpreted in terms of local structure or dynamics.

A manuscript developing exponential-fitting strategies for this combined behavior is in preparation with R. Li and J. H. Ross Jr.

Materials of interest

(Y, Fe)-codoped BaTiO₃ and related multiferroic perovskites; M-type hexaferrites; transition-metal-substituted spinels.

II  ·  Hankuk University of Foreign Studies 2018 — 2024

Soft magnetic composites (SMCs)

Soft magnetic composites are replacing traditional ferrite cores in modern power electronics. As switching frequencies climb past 1 MHz, metallic-particle SMCs become attractive: they retain inductance and permeability at high frequency, and their low coercivity translates into low hysteresis loss.

My work focused on two practical objectives — raising the permeability of the inductor core, which historically lags ferrite, and reducing the total core-loss in the high-frequency band where industry actually operates. The approach combined microstructural deformation, nanopowder additions (Fe–50Ni, Ni, Zn), and particle-size control of Fe–Si–Cr crystalline alloys.

Electromagnetic interference (EMI) shielding

Densification, miniaturisation, and rising operating frequencies have made electromagnetic compatibility a first-order design constraint across mobile devices, IoT, 5G, and automotive electronics. Stray electromagnetic disturbance is now a documented cause of device malfunction — and a public-safety concern.

Our work surveyed candidate shielding materials in the microwave regime: spinel ferrites, M-type hexaferrites including BaFe₁₂₋ₓCoₓO₁₉, and 2-D additives such as MXenes. As 5G transitions from sub-6 to Ka-band (≈ 30 GHz), the design target shifts again — and the electronic structure of the absorber has to be tuned to match.
